I am pleased to write this letter in \textbf{strong support of Enqiang Chen} for admission to your graduate program in Computer Science and related fields. I am Yang Peizhong from the School of Information Science and Engineering at Yunnan University, where my research mainly focuses on spatio-temporal data mining. I have published several first-author papers in well-known venues, including TKDE, TNNLS, and SIGSPATIAL, and I currently serve as the Director of the Computer Science and Technology Degree Program.

I first got to know Enqiang during his sophomore year, when I guided him through some basic readings in data mining and big data. Through our regular discussions about his progress and the difficulties he faced, I gradually saw his growing strength in database systems and statistical analysis. Later, because of his initiative and curiosity, I gave him an undergraduate innovation project and asked him to analyze the problem and come up with a solution on his own. Over the following year, we worked closely on research design, tool selection, and literature reading. In the end, he completed the project on time and obtained a national software copyright.

Enqiang has a strong academic background. He achieved near-perfect scores in Advanced Mathematics and Advanced Algebra, which shows that he has a solid math base and good analytical ability. He also performed very well in major computer science courses such as Database Systems, Application Service Platform Development, and Computer Networks. More importantly, he was able to apply what he learned in practice. For example, he could design back-end database structures according to real business needs, and in network simulation experiments he showed a clear understanding of different network layers.

What impressed me most was his way of thinking about research. He did not just implement existing methods; he tried to understand where earlier approaches fell short and how they could be improved. In our discussions on trajectory pattern mining, he connected theory with algorithm design. He also implemented complex algorithms and carried out experiments in an organized way. He analyzed runtime behavior, improved the data structures, and made the system more stable and efficient. I still remember one stage when the experimental results were not consistent across datasets, which slowed our progress. Instead of getting discouraged, Enqiang went back to the related literature, checked the preprocessing steps again, and adjusted the algorithm design. He kept working through the problem, and the final results became more stable and practically useful. Through this process, he showed a level of maturity and self-learning ability that is not common among undergraduates, and he is capable of reading research papers and turning ideas into actual experiments.

Of course, he is not without weaknesses. At times, he tends to spend too much time on certain tasks and does not always simplify the work as much as he could. In his earlier years, his understanding of data structures was not strong enough, which slowed him down at times. That said, after he spent more time studying core topics such as dynamic programming and tree-based algorithms, his programming thinking and implementation skills improved a lot. In my view, this kind of steady self-improvement is a sign of real growth. In a nutshell, Enqiang Chen is not a perfect student, but he is hardworking, sincere about learning, and genuinely interested in academic exploration; more importantly, he has the ability, persistence, and potential needed for advanced study. \textbf{I therefore recommend him without reservation and believe he will do well in a rigorous graduate environment}.

If you require any further information, please feel free to contact me.
